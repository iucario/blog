It's all too **easy** to copy-paste large passages of AI generated contents, sloppy or not, directly to human readers.

Everyone knows how to ask an AI. When someone asks you a question, they don't need you acting as a lossy interpreter between them and the AI.
Using AI generated contents is absolutely fine. The key to make your content stand out is the **effort** to summarize AI contents and add _your_ perspective for _another human_.

Compared to AI articles, high-quality, human-written contents have **lower entropy**, meaning they have a higher information density.

Lower entropy is undoubtedly a good thing for readers, the internet, and the _universe_.

Reducing entropy requires greater effort. This reflects true respect and intelligence.

I don't like reading long blocks of text, whether they're blogs, documents or Slack messages.
If I spend more than three seconds reading a passage without gaining any new information, I tend to _lose focus_.

**All** tedious stuff is annoying, but AI nonsense is especially irritating.
On the other hand, consuming content carefully crafted by humans is delightful because I can get more information in less time.
**Memes** are a perfect example. They're concise, impactful, and humorous.

It's more **valuable** to keep your output brief than at length.
